# Pre-Call Email Sequence — Base Template (Ecom Coach)

Origin niche: E-commerce / dropshipping coaching program ("Business Belts")
Use: source template for the Pre-Call Email Sequence module. Adapt structure and beats to the lead's niche (fitness coach, UGC coach, etc.) — do not reuse Ecom-specific facts/stats for other niches.

Sequence structure (3 emails):
1. Booking confirmation + call frame ("this call is a yes/no fit check, not a pitch") + pre-call resource link + logistics reminder.
2. Category/objection-busting email ("X is dead" myth) — market data + why the objection is wrong + why now is a good time + why other vehicles don't work for the avatar + CTA to roadmap resource.
3. Member success story — one relatable, "normal" member (not the biggest win) — humanized narrative, specific numbers, direct quote, CTA to call + longer-form resource.

## Template 2 — Category Objection ("X is dead")

Subject line: dropshipping is dead (no it's not)

Every six months for the last decade {{first_name}}, someone on the internet declares e-commerce dead.

Dropshipping is dead. Facebook ads don't work anymore. The market is saturated. It's too late. On and on. And every six months our members do another €10M+ in revenue while the people online keep typing.

The numbers tell a different story than the noise online does.

Global e-commerce in 2019 when I started was around $3.3 trillion. In 2025 it's around $6.8 trillion. The projection for 2027 is north of $8 trillion. The market doubled in six years and it's about to nearly triple.

Saturation means the market is full. The market is not full. It's growing faster than at any point in history.

What people actually mean when they say saturated is that the lazy version is harder than it used to be, and that's true. It's harder than it was in 2019 to throw up a generic store, run cheap ads, and print money with no skill. But the skill ceiling went up, and the skill floor went up too. The people who actually learn this print more than ever.

The second thing nobody talks about is that AI changed the unit economics completely.

Five years ago, to build an e-com business properly you needed a copywriter, a video editor, a designer, a media buyer, plus 30 hours of your own time every week. Today AI does most of that. Not perfectly, but well enough to compress what used to take 30 hours into 2 to 4. That's the only reason our members can run this alongside a full-time job.

Third thing, and this is the one that actually matters. Most other "vehicles" people try don't work for normal people.

Crypto is gambling. You make money some months and lose it back the next. No asset, no compounding. Day trading is the same with extra steps and the math is rigged against you. Real estate needs €50K+ to start and years before you cash flow, which isn't realistic when you're earning €2.500 a month. YouTube and TikTok work but you're either on camera or spending 40 hours a week building something that takes 6+ months to make any money.

E-commerce with the right system is different. You start with €1.000 to €3.000. First sales within days of going live. Real profit within 30 to 90 days. And you build a real asset, a brand, a store, a supplier relationship that actually compounds. The skills you pick up doing it transfer to literally anything you do later, and never become useless.

Not easy though. None of our members would tell you it was easy. But it's the most realistic path to a real second income I've seen in 6+ years of doing this.

The timing right now is better than it was when I started, not worse. More buyers. Better tools. Easier to compress the work. Suppliers fighting for our business because we move volume.

So when someone tells you the market is saturated, they're either selling you something else, or they tried it lazily and quit. The market is not the problem. Doing it without guidance is.

We'll get into your specific situation on Wednesday.

— Thomas

## Template 3 — Relatable Member Story

Subject line: Niels, 41, sales job, €10K days

I want to tell you about Niels {{first_name}}.

If there's one member story I'd want a prospect to read before getting on a call with us, it's his.

Niels is 41, from Rotterdam, married.

He spent 14 to 15 years in corporate sales before he came to us. The kind of job that pays okay, has decent benefits and a company car, and slowly drains the life out of you over a decade.

He'd been looking for something for years before he found us. Other programs. Countless YouTube videos. The books. The Telegram groups. Nothing stuck.

He came to us in December 2024. We had a long call where he was honest with me. He'd been burned before. He wasn't sure if this was different. But he was tired enough to try one more time.

January 1st 2025, he launched his first ads. Two weeks in, his first €1.000 day. (Which is fast even for us. The work he'd put into product research before launching was unusually thorough; he didn't rush it.)

End of month one, he was profitable. Month two was €44.000 in revenue, €9.000 in profit. Month three was €92.000 in revenue.

By October, he was hitting consistent €10K+ revenue days at 25% profit margin.

In 10 months, Niels went from corporate sales to running a business that was making more profit in a single day than his salary made in a month.

He still has the sales job, for now, because he wants to be deliberate about when he leaves. He's not rushing, and he's not desperate. He's just building.

That's the part nobody tells you about doing this properly. When you build alongside a job, every decision is calm. You can take a slow month and not panic. You can test products properly instead of betting the rent money. You can listen to your coach instead of rushing because you "need this to work by Friday".

A few months into working with us, Niels said something to me that I think about a lot. I'm paraphrasing, but it was along the lines of: "For 14 years I was looking for something, the difference here is that someone is actually telling me what to do, every week my coach looks at my store and my ads and tells me the next move, I'm not guessing anymore."

He didn't have a special skill, an E-com background or a computer science degree.

He was a 41-year-old sales guy from Rotterdam who was tired of waiting and decided to start.

I picked Niels for this email instead of Robin's €250K months or Casper and Robbert's €176K months because he's the most normal.

Mid-30s or older. Has a job. Wants out but isn't broke. Sceptical but tired. He's the closest thing we have to the average prospect we get on a call.

If you see yourself in any of that, his path is the realistic version of what's possible. Not a viral overnight story. Just steady work, the right system, and a coach who knew what to fix when things weren't working.

We'll map out what your version of that looks like on Wednesday.

— Thomas
